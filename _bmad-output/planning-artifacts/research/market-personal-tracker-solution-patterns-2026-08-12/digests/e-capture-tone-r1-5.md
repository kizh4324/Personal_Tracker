## PATTERN VERDICTS

- **One-line NLU quick-add (Todoist/TickTick style)** → **proven** → Battle-tested for a decade, shipped in every surface (widget, notification, quick-add bar, voice), and its documented failure modes (false date/word matches, wrong project creation) are handled by tap-to-revert and disable options rather than abandoned. 50-input comparative testing rates it best-in-category. This is the pattern to adopt.
- **Smart date/time parsing in tasks** → **proven** → Universally present, reversible, and its ambiguities are explicitly resolved (most-recent-valid-date rule). Residual complaints (silent default-to-today causing overdue piles) argue for showing the parsed result before commit.
- **Voice-to-task capture (Siri/Assistant/assistant-style, incl. Todoist Ramble)** → **mixed** → Real numbers (76k users, 62% success, 5x upgrades) show demand and viability, and peer-reviewed work shows speech genuinely speeds capture — but Siri's reminder capture is documented as frequently failing, speech adoption has real barriers (unfamiliarity, social context, accuracy tolerance), and all available usage evidence covers only 2–3 week windows. Unproven whether it sustains as a habit vs. novelty; treat as a complementary lane, not the primary capture surface.
- **Streak with recovery (freeze/repair, ≤3-day grace)** → **proven** → First-party Duolingo data (+0.38% DAU from doubling freezes; slack research), plus Finch's 3-day repair and optional streaks, all point the same way: recovery mechanics retain users without destroying streak value. Note: monetizing the freeze is flagged as an anti-pattern.
- **Hard-reset streak (miss = 0)** → **dead-end** → Converging evidence (JCR 2023, LogRocket's 'streak cliff', the Oulu survey's 850-day dropout, multiple practitioner analyses) shows a broken long streak creates quit moments, not restart moments. Avoid as primary mechanism.
- **Punishing HP-loss/gamified-punishment mechanics (Habitica)** → **mixed/dead-end** → Peer-reviewed IJHCS research documents counterproductive effects across all studied users, and the developer shipped an escape valve (Pause Damage). Loss-aversion works for engagement but is shown to backfire on recovery. Don't adopt as core failure tone.
- **Gentle/no-consequence tone (Finch)** → **proven (with a caveat)** → Strong positive sentiment and sustained use in independent reviews, and streak-optionality is cheap to implement. Caveat: one reviewer reports permissiveness weakens the motivational push — design for 'self-chosen short streaks + optional repair,' not pure zero-accountability.
- **Monetized streak protection (paid freeze/repair)** → **dead-end for trust** → Multiple independent analyses converge on it as an adversarial, anxiety-monetizing incentive; earned recovery is the recommended shape.
