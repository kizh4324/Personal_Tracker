## MECHANISM EVIDENCE (cluster 7)

- Implementation intentions / if-then planning: direction positive. Classic effect d=0.65 (94 tests, 2006); freshest 642-test meta gives sample-weighted d=0.36 and publication-bias-adjusted d=0.15 (small). Contingent (if-then) beats schedule-based plans. Counter-evidence: large heterogeneity + publication bias; effect shrinks under robustness analysis.
- JITAI: direction positive but context-dependent. Pre-post meta g=0.77; controlled mental-health meta g=0.15; one fully powered RCT shows OR 1.81 for verified abstinence. Design caveat: people may be unreceptive exactly when support is needed; engagement generally suboptimal.
- Reminders/prompts: direction positive short-term, but decay fast (effect ~3 days), and there is now a clean demonstration of a backfire mode (over-reliance without habit formation, npj Sci Learning 2024) plus a null modality effect and an inconclusive systematic review. Net: reminders move behavior modestly in the near-term; treating them as the core habit mechanism is not supported.
- Zeigarnik: original effect (IR/CR≈2.0, 1927) fails modern replication (2025 meta: no memory advantage; small dz=0.15); only the resumption tendency survives. Do not design around "unfinished tasks nag the memory."

## LEADS

- Notification response/miss studies in HCI: Pielot et al. (2015) "Beyond self-monitoring" and related notification-response-time research — direct evidence for the "missed/dismissed important notifications" premise, NOT captured this run.
- Prospective-memory failure rates in general adult samples (everyday forgetting, missed appointments) — direct "forgetfulness cost" evidence, not found.
- Recent (2021–2026) nationally representative procrastination prevalence (Steel & Klingsieck 2016 review as bridge).
- Effectiveness of task/habit/to-do apps themselves (retention, effect sizes) — directly relevant to the product bet.
- IPSRT/social-rhythm therapy intervention outcomes as precedent for routine-stabilizing features.

## COULD NOT FIND

- Any reliable recent statistic on how often people miss or dismiss important notifications (cluster 2's core premise).
- Direct general-population cost numbers for missed appointments/opportunities.
- A fresh (2021–2026), large, authoritative survey of academic-procrastination prevalence (all recent numbers are secondary citations or self-report student samples).
- A clean prevalence split of behavioral vs decisional procrastination.
- Peer-reviewed effect sizes for generic task/habit management apps (not JITAIs/mHealth-specific).
