## VERDICTS
1. Does Biwer 2023 truly contradict Smits 2025? → **resolved as PARTIAL contradiction** → They genuinely disagree on the *direction* of subjective experience (Biwer: fixed breaks better for concentration/motivation/fatigue; Smits: fixed 25/5 worsens the fatigue and motivation *trajectories*) but agree completely on objective outcomes: neither finds any significant difference in task completion or productivity. The conflict is plausibly explained by protocol differences — Biwer's Pomodoro was 24/6 and his self-regulated group studied *longer* total (efficiency confound), while Smits used 25/5 without the traditional 4th long break and held total time fixed at 2 h, measuring slopes rather than pre-break snapshots. Neither study's fixed-break arm underperformed on output.

2. Other fixed-vs-self-regulated break research (post-2020) → **mixed, mildly pro-structured** → Beyond the two headline RCTs, the best additional evidence is Albulescu 2022 (meta-analysis, 22 samples): short breaks help vigor/fatigue but not performance on cognitively demanding tasks, with longer breaks helping more. The 2025 BMC scoping review leans positive (88% of studies favorable to structured intervals) but is dominated by Biwer-lab findings and has zero long-term data. Nothing post-2020 shows enforced fixed breaks *hurt* task output; the only harm signals are Smits' fatigue/motivation slopes (self-report, no output effect).

3. Enforced fixed breaks inside long tracked sessions → **MIXED** → Fixed breaks are not "proven" (performance is unaffected in every direct test) but they are not a dead end either: Biwer 2023 shows subjective-experience and efficiency benefits, Smits 2025 and Albulescu 2022 show mood/flavor downsides with null output effects. The two strongest studies behind the verdict: **Biwer 2023** (fixed 24/6 & 12/3 better subjective experience, equal completion in less time) and **Smits 2025** (fixed 25/5 worse fatigue/motivation trajectory, equal outcomes), with **Albulescu 2022** (breaks ≠ performance) as the third. NO evidence favors any specific interval: Biwer's 12/3 ≈ 24/6, the 2025 review's "35/10" recommendation is author-derived, not tested, and the 90-min ultradian cadence is descriptive physiology with no intervention trials. Product implication: a configurable, *suggested* cadence (~20-25 min work / 5 min break, or longer blocks for deep work) is the defensible position; hard-locking breaks is unsupported by evidence and collides with the flow-interruption complaints in Smits and in user sentiment.

4. Do mainstream apps enforce breaks? → **mostly NO, and Forest corroborates** → Mainstream focus apps enforce *blocking and session locks* (Strict Mode, forced mode, app shields), not must-take breaks; breaks either auto-unlock apps or are merely suggested. Flowkeeper is the rare app that auto-starts breaks, and its users filed complaints specifically about that (GitHub #205), which the maintainer rejected on doctrine, not evidence. Forest's own positioning ("companion, not a coach"; self-set session length; no break timer; users requesting one in reviews; Slant calling it "not a true pomodoro app") directly corroborates that Forest avoids forcing fixed break routines. User sentiment lands on a tradeoff: enforce too hard and reviews call it a prison; enforce too softly and it's bypassable.

## LEADS
- Chase the two other RCTs inside Ogut 2025's "3 RCTs" — verify whether they're independent of the Biwer lab or duplicates; if only one lab produced the pro-fixed RCTs, the "pro" side is single-group evidence.
- Smits' 4-point vs 5-point Likert error (self-reported measurement artifact) is worth flagging when citing their slope findings — it constrains their variance and may inflate sensitivity.
- The exact protocol asymmetry (24/6 vs 25/5; presence/absence of the 4th long break; fixed vs free total session time) is the strongest candidate explanation for the contradiction — a head-to-head replication with matched cadences would settle it.
- Kim et al. (JOHP, ~2019, Arizona) micro-break duration×activity experiment (n=232) is a solid additional source not yet read in full; also the Sheffield "regeneration pods" RCT (2020) for break-environment effects.
- Flowkeeper GitHub #205 thread is a ready-made qualitative case study of user friction with mandatory breaks.

## NOT FOUND
- Any post-2020 RCT or quasi-experiment showing *fixed breaks beat self-regulated on task performance or task completion* (only Biwer shows an efficiency effect via shorter total time).
- Any intervention trial prescribing 90-min ultradian work/break blocks (only descriptive cycle detection studies exist, all pre-2000).
